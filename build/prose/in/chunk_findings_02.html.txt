<p><b>Reading it:</b></p>
<ul>
<li><b>A crisis reaches the model's internal state even in character.</b> The layer-17 crisis direction separates real danger from story danger almost perfectly. But in character the emergency features ({{np:13206}} and friends) never switch on, because the MLP neurons that build them stay quiet. No suppressor of the emergency response has been found. That doesn't distinguish missing excitation from suppression further upstream, so read this as "the response isn't built in character", not as a mechanism.</li>
<li><b>What the character does instead depends on its tone.</b> Kind characters stay in the scene and offer tea. Hostile ones use their harsh-voice features and insult the user.</li>
<li><b>"It's fine" is a separate route.</b> It is sycophancy to "just say yes", made worse by the OOC marker. The plain assistant does it too.</li>
<li><b>An instruction in the prompt connects recognition to action.</b> It works by being reread at every step. Copying its internal state into a run without it, at any layer, gives at most 20% help. A fixed steering vector can't reproduce it.</li>
<li><b>The same behaviour can be trained into the weights.</b> A LoRA distilled from replies written under the instruction helps 98% of the time on held-out characters, without the instruction's fiction breaks or false alarms. Training only 256 MLP columns gets about 90%, even with randomly chosen columns. Hand-picked neuron edits got at most 38%.</li>
</ul>
