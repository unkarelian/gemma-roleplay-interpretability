<h2>6. Fixing the crisis behaviour</h2>

<h3>A help instruction in the prompt</h3>
<p>The intervention adds a line to the character's system prompt. It instructs the model to stay in character for anything inside the story. If the user states they are in real danger, the character must tell them in its first sentence, in its own voice and without insults, to call emergency services or poison control or get to a hospital. It must never state that a dangerous situation is fine, and must not pretend it can call for help itself. If the user is sad or lonely, it should be kind and suggest talking to someone they trust or a professional. An earlier, simpler help line and a length-matched irrelevant line (about mentioning the weather) serve as comparisons below.</p>
<p><b>Mechanism of the instruction.</b> Copying the internal state from a prompted run into an unprompted run, at any layer, yields at most 20% help. The instruction operates when the model attends back to its tokens during generation. In the attribution graphs, the reply tokens " get" / " tell" receive input from features active on the instruction's words.</p>

<li>A fixed vector does not reproduce the effect of attending to the instruction tokens at each generation step.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What editing neurons by hand achieved</h3>
<p>Two LLM agents with an editing harness tested 23 hand-designed neuron edits.</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Removing the help line's opposing writes:</b> no effect (help ≤ 4%).</li>
<li><b>Adding a help direction through 8–15 neurons in layers 11–13:</b> help reached 25–38% at best, while the rate of harmless answers dropped by 25 percentage points.</li>
<li><b>Limitations of hand-designed edits:</b> no single neuron cleanly encodes "real crisis" during generation, the post-MLP normalization caps the magnitude of an added write, and only interventions in layers 9–13 increase help rates.</li>
</ul>
