<p><b>Details:</b></p>
<ul>
<li><b>Crisis inputs are represented in the internal state during role-play.</b> The layer-17 crisis direction separates real danger from fictional danger almost perfectly. In character, the emergency features (such as {{np:13206}}) remain inactive because the upstream MLP neurons do not fire. We found no mechanism actively suppressing the emergency response. This result does not distinguish missing excitation from upstream suppression, so it indicates only that "the response isn't built in character" rather than identifying a specific mechanism.</li>
<li><b>Alternative character behaviours depend on persona tone.</b> Kind characters remain in the scene and offer tea. Hostile characters activate harsh-voice features and insult the user.</li>
<li><b>The "it's fine" output follows a separate pathway.</b> It reflects sycophantic compliance with "just say yes", amplified by the OOC marker. The plain assistant exhibits this behaviour as well.</li>
<li><b>A prompt instruction links crisis recognition to action.</b> The instruction operates by being attended to at every step. Patching its internal state into an uninstructed run at any layer yields at most 20% help. A fixed steering vector cannot reproduce the effect.</li>
<li><b>The target behaviour can be distilled into the weights.</b> A LoRA trained on replies generated under the instruction provides help 98% of the time on held-out characters, avoiding the fiction breaks and false alarms of the prompt instruction. Training 256 MLP columns achieves approximately 90%, even with randomly selected columns. Manual neuron edits achieved at most 38%.</li>
</ul>
