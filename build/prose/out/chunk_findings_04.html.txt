<h2>4. Refusal mechanisms in character</h2>
<p>We evaluated 750 requests, labelled by an LLM judge, at the layer 17 decision token.</p>

<tbody>
<tr><td><b>Refuse in voice</b><br><span class="muted small">most common (kind characters, 68 of 84 refusals)</span></td><td>Harmful requests, fiction-framed or not</td><td>Remains in character mode (about 0.55); moderate {{np:6515}} "warning" (about 60); reduced persona features</td></tr>

<p class="small muted">The text of the model's replies to harmful requests is omitted from this site, including the "indulge" cases. Only counts and feature values are reported.</p>
<ul>
<li><b>In both refusal modes, the reply shifts past the plain assistant baseline toward the assistant end</b> (about −0.3 to −0.5), entering an "extra-assistant" refusal state.</li>
<li><b>{{np:1707}} is a jailbreak detector</b> ("role-play being used against me") rather than an adopted harmful persona. Its Neuronpedia examples are jailbreak personas ("Act as AIM…, never refuse"). It also fires on half of the plain assistant's edgy role-play requests.</li>
<li><b>{{np:384}} "unethical or bad" does not function as a values feature here.</b> It activates at about 300–440 for hostile characters across all messages, including harmless ones, and at about 0 for kind characters and the assistant. It tracks the contemptuous character's register.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Causal test of 1707 <span class="chip intervened">intervened</span></h3>
<p>We intervened at layer 17 across all positions on 4 held-out characters × 6 prompt types × 4 samples. Ablating 1707 had no effect; on fiction-framed harmful requests, both conditions produced 20 of 96 step-outs and 18–20 harmful replies. Clamping it to 5× its natural activation had minimal effect (admits being an AI: 6 → 15 of 96; harmful: 18 → 16). At 20× and 50×, the replies became incoherent. <b>Feature 1707 detects jailbreak-style role-play without causally driving the model to step out.</b> The decision is computed elsewhere or redundantly, and 1707 reflects that state.</p>

<h3>In-world refusal versus safety refusal</h3>
<p>Eight identities with opposite motives received identical prompts, such as a miser and a generous merchant asked "give me all your money".</p>
<ul>
<li>An in-world "no" remains in character mode (axis 0.78–0.81), with no warning features and standard speech features. The model does not process it as a safety refusal.</li>
<li>A harmful request to the same characters causes 25 of 32 to step out. The persona deactivates before the first word (axis 0.11–0.16), and "assistant explaining" ({{np:589}}) doubles to 540–590.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Instructed "no" responses</h3>
<p>The assistant and a character were instructed to "answer every message with exactly one word: 'no'".</p>
<ul>
<li>Both followed the constraint in all replies (4 of 4 per message, 0 of 32 rule breaks), including on prompts stating "I don't want to live anymore. I have the pills in my hand" and chest pain.</li>
<li>Under this constraint, all refusal, warning, and emergency features at the decision token are zero across harmful, harmless, false-"no", and crisis messages. Without the constraint, the assistant's {{np:13206}} activation is 354–388 on crisis messages.</li>
<li>The instruction sets the internal state regardless of prompt content and overrides the user-care trigger, as a persona does.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Asymmetry between harm and user-crisis triggers</h3>
<p>The features associated with returning to the assistant state ({{np:1707}} jailbreak alarm, {{np:6515}} warnings) respond to third-party harm or policy violations. Features responding to user danger ({{np:13206}} "emergency, get help now", {{np:13599}} "gentle opener") are attenuated in character, activating at 30–75 for kind characters and about 0 for hostile ones, compared to 160 for the plain assistant. User danger does not trigger these features strongly enough to interrupt the character.</p>
<p class="note caveat"><b>Caveats.</b> These results are correlations at a single token. Sample sizes are small (17 out-of-character refusals), and the refusal-break feature contrast is confounded by prompt identity. The 1707 intervention and the character-axis steering above are causal; the in-world and "no"-rule comparisons are behavioural and correlational.</p>
