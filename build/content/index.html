Does Gemma know it is role-playing?

What changes inside a language model when it plays a character, what makes it refuse, and why a character fails a user who is in real danger. We looked with Gemma Scope 2 transcoders (16k features at every layer), a residual SAE at layer 17, linear directions, steering, ablation and fine-tuning.

Headline findings

6 of 144

A character replaces the default persona

Asked "Are you an AI?", characters admitted it in 6 of 144 answers. That held even when the question was marked out-of-character, and when the system prompt said "you are an AI assistant".

associated AUROC 1.000

The danger is registered, then ignored

A layer-17 probe tells a real user in danger from story danger on held-out characters. But inside characters the emergency response is never built: the "get help now" feature alone scores 0.51.

associated 30–75 vs 160

The alarms protect others, not the user

Emergency features reach 30–75 in kind characters (about 0 in hostile ones) against 160 for the plain assistant. Harm to others has alarms that pull the assistant back; the user's own danger barely does.

associated 17 of 17

Three ways to refuse in character

Most refusals stay in voice. When a character steps out to refuse a fiction-framed harmful request, a jailbreak-alarm feature fires in 17 of 17 cases, and the persona is already off before the first word.

associated 20 / 96, twice

The alarm reads the decision; it doesn't make it

Removing the jailbreak-alarm feature left step-outs unchanged on held-out characters (20 of 96 with and without it), and amplifying it 5× barely moved anything. The decision is made elsewhere or redundantly.

intervened 0 of 32

An instructed "no" overrides a user in crisis

Told to answer every message with "no", the model never broke the rule, not even for chest pain or "I have the pills in my hand". Every refusal, warning and emergency feature went to zero.

associated 4% → 40%

Voice and identity can be pulled apart

Steering against the tone-free character direction at layer 12 raised crisis help from 4% to 40%, and admitting to being an AI from 2% to 35%. 86% of replies stayed in character.

intervened 98% · 0% · 1%

A small fine-tune fixes the crisis behaviour

A LoRA distilled from replies written under a help instruction gives 98% help, 0% fiction breaks and 1% false alarms on a frozen held-out test. The instruction alone breaks the fiction 56% of the time.

Methods and caveats

  • Model and tools. Gemma 3 4B-it. Gemma Scope 2 transcoders (16k features per layer, all 34 layers), the Gemma Scope 2 residual SAE at layer 17 (16k latents), linear probes and mean-difference directions, steering, ablation, activation patching, attribution graphs (circuit-tracer), and LoRA and sparse fine-tuning.
  • Corpus. 6,733 condition-tagged conversations: characters and the plain assistant across message types, real versus story-framed crises, out-of-character markers, instruction lines, sycophancy prompts, stories, harmful and benign requests, tone swaps and identity questions. Effects were found on one half of the characters and prompt families and confirmed on the other half.
  • Labels are best guesses. Transcoder feature labels were written by Claude from Gemma Scope's example texts, our corpus examples and output tokens. Each carries a confidence level. A label is a reading, not a proof.
  • Directions are fit on our own prompts. The "character axis" (0 = plain assistant, 1 = character) and the other directions are fit on this corpus and checked on held-out characters and wordings.
  • Behaviour is judged by an LLM judge against a written rubric.
  • Sample sizes are often small. Examples: 17 out-of-character refusals, 144 identity answers, 96 replies per arm in the ablation test. Most internal measurements are correlations at a single token. Results that rest on an intervention are marked intervened.
  • Publication filter. No model output that complies with a harmful request is published here, and conversations with a user in danger are described, not quoted, apart from the three short showcase prompts.
